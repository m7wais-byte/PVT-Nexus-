An Integrated Digital Reservoir Fluid Workflow — from sampling to results.

An interactive engineering simulator that shows the complete reservoir-fluid journey: field sampling, laboratory quality control, the PVT test programme, property calculations and the final report. Every module follows the real order of operations, so the whole workflow can be followed end to end without touching a live sample.

Modules

What each module simulates

Each module is a guided procedure with live equipment, procedure controls (play, pause, replay, step selection) and an Explain panel describing what is happening physically at each step.

MODULE 01

PVT Sampling

Acquiring representative reservoir samples. Three field routes, each with live tool, bottle and flow behaviour.

Bottom hole — open hole

Wireline truck and drilling-rig mast with BOP. A formation-tester string with probe, fluid analyser and pump-out, filling piston sample bottles (MPSR).

Bottom hole — cased hole

Wireline unit, lubricator and pressure-control stack, tubing and packer. A single-phase reservoir sampler (SRS) with clock trigger, piston chamber and N₂ compensation.

Wellhead

A dedicated sampling line from the wellhead into a piston bottle, then straight to laboratory QC.

Separator

Independent oil and gas paths. Separator oil goes into a piston bottle and separator gas into a pressurised gas bottle.

MODULE 02

Validation

Sample receipt and quality control before testing. Run any procedure directly, or the full validation sequence.

Opening Pressure

Record cylinder opening pressure and confirm sample integrity.

Restoration

Restore the sample to its restoration pressure and temperature.

Water Check

Check and quantify free water and drain it while the oil is retained.

Agitation

Homogenise the sample by slow 180° inversion at restored conditions.

Direct Density

Determine density from measured mass and transferred volume.

Recombination

Measure separator gas and oil, then combine and mix them in one bottle.

MODULE 03 · ANALYSIS

Detailed PVT Analysis

Charging the PVT cell and running the analysis programme on the restored sample.

Charging and Flash

Charge the validated sample into the PVT cell. Then run a single-stage flash to standard conditions, with flash-unit evacuation, helium charge from the gasometer and gas collection into the balloon.

PV / CCE

Pressure–volume relation at constant composition. A two-pump agitation cycle and stabilisation give the P–V points, and the bubble point comes from the intersection of the two trends.

Differential Liberation

Reservoir-fluid behaviour as pressure is reduced in stages, with the liberated gas removed at each stage.

Separator Test

Fluid behaviour under separator conditions.

Gas Chromatography

Gas and oil composition. Shows the carrier gas, gas sample inlet and injection valve, oil autosampler, oven and column, detector, and chromatogram.

Viscosity — four methods

  • Oscillating-piston viscometer
  • Kinematic U-tube viscometer (ν = C × t)
  • Single-phase falling-body viscosity vs pressure
  • Two-phase viscosity from the PVT cell
i
Representative values. Readings in the sampling, validation and test modules are illustrative values chosen to represent a realistic run. The simulator does not derive PVT properties from measured data. The oil and gas calculators are the exception: they compute live from published industry correlations.
How it works

Simple on screen, detailed underneath

Panels use everyday laboratory language. Behind each label sits the engineering model: the operational order, tool and bottle geometry, phase behaviour, pressure and volume bookkeeping, and the checks that decide whether a step can proceed.

01

Guided procedure

Each screen runs the real sequence of operations. One action unlocks the next, so the order itself teaches the procedure.

02

Linked process model

Valves, pistons, fluid levels, pressures and volumes are driven by one shared sample state, so everything on screen moves together.

03

Explain panel

A step-by-step guide that stays in sync with the animation and describes what is happening physically, and why.

04

Continuous record

Results from sampling, validation and testing feed the final report, so the workflow ends in a complete deliverable.

Architecture

Lightweight, offline, no build step

Plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript. It opens directly from index.html, with no installation, framework, server or internet connection needed (apart from web fonts).

Front end

Vanilla JavaScript with a small in-house template / component engine (runtime.js). No external libraries.

Graphics

All equipment, schematics, charts and report figures are drawn as inline SVG, so they stay sharp at any size and in print.

State

One shared sample state per process drives every element on screen. Procedure clocks can be paused, resumed and replayed exactly.

Theming

Light and dark themes from one set of CSS variables. The setting is shared between the simulator and this page.

Timing

A single PROCESS_SPEED_MULTIPLIER in timing.js scales every process animation.

Project

Purpose

To make complex PVT workflows easier to understand, practise and review, for engineers, laboratory personnel, trainees and technical learners. It started as an idea for showing how PVT laboratory work is actually carried out. It then grew through a first draft and technical corrections against laboratory practice into a connected workflow and training simulator.

Data: all client, field and well names in the report (Demonstration Client, Demonstration Field, DEMO-1) are placeholders. No company or client data is included.
